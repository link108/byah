# Series: Shared Language and AI

Source: `chatgpt-2026-08-19-series-plan.md` — a ChatGPT chat starting from Pantone and open-source color projects, generalizing to "shared language as economic/social infrastructure," then speculating about AI's effect on that.

## What shipped vs. what the chat proposed

Posts 1 and 2 follow the chat closely (Pantone as coordination protocol; shared vocabulary as a precondition for a marketplace) — that material was solid and Cameron didn't push back on it.

Post 3 is **not** the chat's proposed conclusion. The chat's article 3 ended on "AI is becoming an invisible standards body for language" — a story entirely about vocabulary (translation, homogenization, machine dialects). Cameron flagged that this wasn't where he was going: he wanted both positive and negative angles on AI/culture, plus a point the chat never raised at all — that AI-personalized content removes the *shared object* itself, not just the shared word for it (his example: everyone watching the same TV show enables a real cultural moment; an LLM generating a unique show per viewer removes that, since there's no "it" to discuss).

After talking it through, the agreed structure for post 3: **shared vocabulary vs. shared experience** as two distinct things AI can fragment, each explored with a genuine downside and a genuine upside, not just "AI bad." The vocabulary half uses the chat's material. The experience half (the shared-object argument, its downside, and the two candidate upsides — niche/minority taste finally getting served, and the shared thing possibly relocating from the artifact to the practice around it) is original synthesis built from Cameron's one example, not something he wrote out — **this is the part most likely to not fully match his actual view and worth reading closely.**

## Posts

1. **`pantone-is-a-protocol`** — Pantone Is a Protocol. Pantone's product is the reduction of ambiguity, not the colors themselves; the 2022-23 Adobe/Pantone licensing dispute as what happens when a shared coordination layer has an owner. (Adobe/Pantone dates and the Design Tokens W3C stable-spec date were verified via web search before use.)
2. **`you-need-a-dictionary-before-a-marketplace`** — You Need a Dictionary Before You Get a Marketplace. Shared vocabulary enables discovery/comparison/compatibility/reputation/automation/specialization; open standards aren't automatically better than proprietary ones, adoption matters more than purity.
3. **`shared-vocabulary-shared-experience`** — the reframed conclusion described above.
